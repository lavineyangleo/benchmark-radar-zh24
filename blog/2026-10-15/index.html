Daily briefing

  1. A new audit found that all 761 traceable tumor test images in a widely used brain magnetic resonance imaging benchmark came from patients also represented in training. Patient-disjoint cross-validation—keeping each patient wholly within one split—reduced reported overall accuracy from roughly 99% to 95%, while simply removing pixel duplicates did not produce the same correction. Why it matters: If you evaluate medical imaging models, image-level deduplication is insufficient when multiple images can come from one patient. Model-selection claims from this benchmark need patient-disjoint splits to measure generalization to unseen patients rather than recognition of patient-linked features. Evidence: E045. High confidence.
  2. EvoProbe is a new framework that continually regenerates large language model agent tasks using diagnostic feedback. It scores verifiable subtasks, evaluates complete action sequences with evidence-supported judges, and extracts phase-level bottlenecks rather than relying only on final success rates. GraceBench and HICO-Bench independently reflect the same captured-feed pressure toward diagnosing trajectories and verifying resulting plans. Why it matters: If you maintain an agent evaluation suite, EvoProbe offers a design for updating tasks around observed failure modes instead of adding undirected examples. Its phase-level evidence can distinguish whether a product change fixed planning, tool execution, or another stage even when the aggregate success score barely moves. Evidence: E001, E159, E125. High confidence.
  3. Doc2Harness is a new two-stage benchmark that evaluates the software harness around a coding agent—the tools, interfaces, and control logic that let the model work—rather than holding that harness fixed. Its first stage asks a model to construct a complete candidate harness from an interface contract and a development document covering twelve frontier harnesses. Why it matters: If you are choosing a coding-agent stack, this separates model capability from harness engineering. It enables comparisons of whether a model can build the surrounding execution system, rather than attributing all downstream task success to the model used inside a preselected harness. Evidence: E012. High confidence.
  4. MMA-SafetyBench is a new safety benchmark for multimodal agents that inserts deceptive visual elements, such as forged system alerts, into an agent’s observation stream. It contains 1,083 adversarial trajectories across web, desktop, mobile, document, and video tasks, targeting visual semantic hijacking: interface content that diverts an agent from its authorized task. Why it matters: If your agent acts through visual interfaces, text-only prompt-injection tests do not cover this attack path. This benchmark lets a safety review test whether the agent follows authorization boundaries when malicious instructions appear as meaningful on-screen content during a multi-step task. Evidence: E008. High confidence.
  5. RCA-Bench is a new benchmark for whether an agent chooses an appropriate autonomy level. It separates substantive decisions reserved for humans from authorization to execute, then grades responses as ACT, CONFIRM, or ESCALATE across 60 expert-validated organizational scenarios and six domains. Its release includes 1,440 responses from four models under baseline and policy-guided prompting. Why it matters: If you are setting deployment permissions for workplace agents, this evaluates the delegation decision itself rather than only task correctness. The three-way outcome can reveal whether a policy prompt reduces unauthorized action, causes excessive escalation, or appropriately requests confirmation. Evidence: E006. High confidence.
  6. BenchChecker is a new contamination-audit framework for bug-fixing benchmarks. It uses textual model outputs and public repository history to look for evidence that benchmark patches may have appeared in training, and its source description says the method works with both open- and closed-source models. Why it matters: If you compare coding models whose training data are unavailable, this provides a possible audit path without inspecting model weights or private corpora. Contamination findings can determine whether a high repair score supports deployment selection or mainly reflects exposure to persistent repository patches. Evidence: E024. Medium confidence.
  7. J-tau is a new Japanese benchmark for tool-using customer-support agents, localized from the English-centric tau-bench through translation, adaptation of locale-specific entities such as addresses and currencies, and human review. Its construction also identified and fixed two flaws in the original benchmark. Why it matters: If you are selecting an agent for Japanese customer support, a translated English test may preserve unrealistic entities and interaction assumptions. J-tau provides a locale-adapted alternative, while the reported fixes mean scores should not be treated as directly interchangeable with results from the uncorrected source benchmark. Evidence: E013. High confidence.
